# Goal-specific training blocks

Retains the primary skill's goal-led periodization and integrates secondary planning and execution feedback. Do not force a fixed phase length or distribution onto every athlete.

## Build the week from actual work

Read current availability, existing calendar, last 2–4 weeks of actual sessions and the longer baseline where needed. A strategy or new block generally benefits from about 6–8 weeks of history. Include strength and other sports when they materially affect recovery. Reuse stated constraints rather than re-interviewing the athlete.

State the block objective and current limiter with evidence. For solo sustained speed, develop sustainable power, time at that power, durability, pacing and aerodynamic position. Do not promise a speed from FTP alone; use [route/pacing guidance](race-and-pacing.md).

## Select the dose

- **Endurance:** sustainable aerobic volume and durability with relatively low recovery cost. The long ride should not quietly turn into a third hard day.
- **Tempo / SS:** sustained subthreshold work; useful for accumulating specific work with a different fatigue cost from threshold. It is quality work, not recovery.
- **Threshold:** repeatable work near the practical threshold anchor, increasing sustainable output and time-to-exhaustion. Train it rather than repeatedly testing to failure.
- **VO₂-focused intervals:** hard aerobic work whose appropriate power depends on duration and athlete response. FTP percentages are a starting point, not proof of attaining VO₂max. A 4×8 label does not prescribe one universal %FTP or HR zone.
- **Anaerobic / sprint:** use when the actual goal requires short power and repeated surges, not automatically for a steady cruising goal.

Adaptations overlap. A low-volume athlete may benefit from SS and threshold, a pyramidal mixture or a VO₂ block; do not declare sweet spot universally best or insist on a rigid 80/20 split. State whether distribution is time-based, session-based and in a 3-zone physiological or 7-zone power system before comparing percentages.

## Progress and recovery

Keep a recognizable pattern for several weeks, with conditional progressions and a review date. Progress one major variable (rep duration, total work, power or weekly volume) at a time. Compare actual completion, not merely scheduled TSS. Distinguish adding six work minutes from adding an entire high-intensity day.

For an athlete riding three times weekly, one quality + one endurance + one long endurance is a baseline option; two separated quality sessions + a genuinely easy long ride can be appropriate when delivered work and recovery support it. Do not freeze either structure as a rule. Separate demanding days by recovery where feasible; if availability forces consecutive days, prioritize the key session and reduce the adjacent dose rather than stacking all missed work.

Plan easier weeks from accumulated fatigue and calendar demands; a 2–3-week build followed by a lighter week is an option, not an age-based mandate. Do not chase a prescribed positive TSB or CTL ramp. Show predicted load and its limitations when using it to justify progression.

Strength supports cycling but creates its own fatigue. Coordinate demanding leg work with key rides and individual DOMS response; low-cadence riding is not a proven substitute for gym strength. Do not add a hundred squat reps simply because legs were a previous limiter.

## Deliverable

Use the format requested: concise chat, calendar events, or an HTML/multiweek artifact if asked. Do not require extra YAML/HTML for a calendar adjustment. Every concrete session states total duration, stages, power zones and subranges, RPE, useful cadence and a fallback. Check arithmetic, spacing and duplicates, then use the authorized [calendar workflow](intervals-icu-guide.md).
