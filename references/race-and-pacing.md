# Sustained speed and event pacing

Integrates the secondary pacing/event modules into the primary goal-specific coaching workflow. An event plan is conditional on course, duration, training and conditions, not a fixed IF table.

## Solo cruising goals

Define the target speed, duration and conditions, then progress from a shorter continuous effort toward the goal duration on a safe comparable route. Distinguish road average, active moving average, uninterrupted cruising and drafting-assisted speed. Report pause exclusion explicitly. Do not infer sustainable one-hour speed from a short maximum or a route with many traffic stops.

Use repeated measured-power sections at comparable speed, direction, position, wind and temperature when possible. Outdoors, wind resistance depends on air-relative velocity; still-air aerodynamic power approximately scales with speed cubed, rolling-resistance power roughly linearly with ground speed. Account for grade, acceleration/braking, bike+rider mass, drivetrain efficiency, CdA, Crr and uncertainty. Round-trip routes do not make wind disappear. Do not fit CdA/Crr/wind as if all were separately identifiable from sparse ride means.

Report the estimated power range needed at target speed and how it compares with measured sustainable power and expected fatigue, not one exact FTP requirement or guaranteed training deadline. A stable seated position, steady pacing, good tires/pressure and safe handling can affect speed alongside physiological improvement. Cadence should be comfortable and sustainable; pushing a lower gear is an option to manage torque, not proof of better efficiency.

## Events

When a real event exists, clarify priority (A/B/C), course, expected duration, weather and fueling opportunities. Prioritize A events in the block; use B/C events as benchmarks or training when appropriate. A cruising goal is not automatically a race requiring a two-week taper.

Choose power budgets from demonstrated duration-specific ability. Avoid generic all-day climbing prescriptions near FTP. On rolling routes allocate effort without repeated unsustainable surges, and use W′ models only with adequate calibration. Give a realistic start, steady phase and finish strategy with contingencies. HR lags and is a response check, not an instantaneous pacing target.

Tapers reduce accumulated fatigue while retaining some intensity, but duration and reduction depend on prior volume, fatigue and event. Do not require a specific positive TSB or exact 14-day schedule for every athlete. Use actual taper response from past comparable events when available. For current weather, consult a live forecast; do not bake seasonal assumptions into guaranteed performance predictions.
