# Load-led planning and current recovery

Combine primary-skill data-first coaching with the secondary skill's readiness workflow. Fixed population thresholds are prompts for inspection, not automatic decisions.

## Load frame

1. Read **fresh actual CTL, ATL and Form**, plus roughly 7–14 days of trajectory and recent completed load. State source, date and whether Form is absolute or percentage. Investigate differences between cached STAS summaries and direct Intervals values; prefer a dated, clearly identified source rather than silently blending them.
2. Confirm the load basis: power TSS, HRSS, estimated power, threshold changes, cross-sport weighting and duplicate uploads. Read sensor-history transitions from the athlete profile or activity provenance rather than assuming all historical power is equally reliable.
3. Read upcoming sessions and forecast load **before and after the proposed change**, including the weekend and recovery spacing. Distinguish projected end-of-day Form from pre-session readiness; a model forecast is conditional on completing the scheduled dose and any platform load overrides.
4. Compare the candidate week with the athlete's recent sustainable weeks, quality minutes and actual recovery. CTL targets, weekly percent increments and absolute TSB bands are not validated universal limits. A short-lived modest negative Form can be compatible with productive training; positive Form is not permission to add intensity indefinitely.

## Recovery check

Use the latest report on leg heaviness, soreness, general fatigue and motivation, plus sleep, resting HR and comparable HRV trends when present. HRV missingness is not normal HRV; Apple sporadic SDNN and nightly RMSSD are not interchangeable. Avoid treating one low sleep score or a few bpm as a diagnosis. Compare multiple days with the athlete's baseline and measurement conditions.

Soreness can diminish through adaptation, less muscle-damaging work or better recovery. CTL/ATL/Form cannot establish why soreness disappeared. Do not preserve a restriction based on obsolete soreness after the user reports improvement; check recent execution and current function instead. Conversely, no soreness does not prove full recovery.

## Choose an action

| Evidence pattern | Reasonable action |
|---|---|
| Load within recently tolerated range, key sessions completed, recovery normal | Execute the planned dose; consider one modest progression if it serves the block |
| Mixed signals or execution deteriorating | Keep intensity but trim work minutes, or replace quality with endurance; explain what drove the choice |
| Marked current functional impairment, illness or concerning exertional symptoms | Stop the intensity plan and respond proportionately to current symptoms; do not use a favorable TSB to overrule them |

These patterns are judgement guides, not a numerical traffic-light diagnostic. Do not infer overtraining syndrome or autonomic dysfunction from TSB, HR suppression or HRV alone. Do not repeatedly foreground an old medical condition when it does not explain the current decision; apply existing clinician restrictions when known.

## Same-day execution check

Include a warmup check and an explicit easier option. If the first rep is near maximal or the lower target cannot be maintained with normal technique, shorten/reduce remaining work. Do not require a particular HR during the first minutes, encourage surges to correct an average, or force a high cadence when it increases strain. The rest day or lower dose is a training adjustment, not failure.
