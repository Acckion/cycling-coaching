# Cycling fuel, recovery and body composition

Adapted from the primary nutrition reference and secondary fueling guidance. Quantities are starting ranges, adjusted to duration, intensity, meals, gut tolerance, weather and the athlete's goals; they are not mandatory prescriptions for every ride. Verify niche or consequential claims with authoritative sports-nutrition sources when needed.

## Practical fueling

- Short easy rides after normal meals may not need carbohydrate during the ride.
- For roughly 1–2.5 h of purposeful riding, a starting range around 30–60 g carbohydrate/h is often useful; use the higher end for demanding work and adjust for prior meals and tolerance.
- Long demanding events may justify 60–90 g/h with practiced mixed-carbohydrate intake. Do not prescribe 90–120 g/h by default to a novice doing two easy hours. Higher intakes require individualized rationale and gut practice.
- Start eating early enough to avoid depletion; distribute intake. Use familiar food, gels or drinks and count grams rather than naming products alone.
- Drink to conditions, thirst and individual sweat losses. Generic hourly fluid/sodium ranges are examples, not fixed requirements; do not encourage forced overdrinking. Specify whether sodium is mg/h or mg/L.

## Recovery and daily intake

Provide adequate energy and carbohydrate around key sessions. Spread protein across meals; a common training starting range is about 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day when appropriate, adjusted to diet and goals. A normal recovery meal can meet needs; do not impose a mandatory shake or a universal 30-minute window. Faster carbohydrate replacement matters most when the next demanding session is soon.

For calorie estimates distinguish mechanical kJ, metabolic kcal, active calories and total daily needs. Measured cycling work in kJ can approximately resemble metabolic kcal because of efficiency, but the units are not interchangeable and individual efficiency varies. Do not add exercise calories twice by combining a training-inclusive activity multiplier with the full workout again.

For fat loss or recomposition, protect fueling for training, monitor body-mass trend, hunger, performance and recovery, and use a modest adjustable energy deficit where appropriate. Do not claim local exercises selectively remove abdominal or thigh fat. Food-photo/restaurant estimates need portion/oil uncertainty ranges rather than false precision. Do not prescribe supplements or deficiencies from training metrics alone.
